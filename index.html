How a nor’easter forms (shown for prototype only)

A nor’easter forms when all four of these line up:

  1. The trough sits over the East Coast: between . Further west, the storm goes inland. Further east, it goes out to sea.
  2. The cold and warm air meet in the right place. A storm forms only where the continental air and the ocean air meet, along the jet on the trough’s east side. For a nor’easter, that line has to cross the coast between the Gulf and Cape Cod; for an inland storm, it has to lie over land; for a storm out to sea, over open ocean.
